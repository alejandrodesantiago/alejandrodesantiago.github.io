I am a Postdoctoral Research Associate in the Bik Lab in the Institute of Bioinformatics and the Department of Marine Sciences at the University of Georgia. I earned my Ph.D. in Bioinformatics from the University of Georgia under the supervision of Dr. Holly Bik, where I developed computational workflows for analyzing single-specimen metagenomic sequencing datasets to characterize microbial symbioses and identify novel chemoautotrophic symbionts of marine nematodes. My research integrates bioinformatics, comparative genomics, and microbial ecology to investigate the diversity and evolution of host-associated microbes.
I received my B.S. in Microbiology from the University of California, Riverside, where I conducted undergraduate research in the Bik Lab. I was also an HHMI Exceptional Research Opportunities Program (ExROP) Fellow in the Jez Lab at Washington University in St. Louis, where I investigated the kinetic and structural properties of chorismate mutase, a protein secreted by plant-parasitic nematodes during the early stages of host infection.
My long-term research goal is to understand the evolutionary processes that shape host–microbe interactions and to develop computational approaches that enable the discovery and characterization of microbial diversity from complex environmental sequencing datasets.
